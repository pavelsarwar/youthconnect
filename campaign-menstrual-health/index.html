Why This Matters

Dignity should not depend on geography.

Remote communities can face barriers to affordable menstrual products, trusted health information and regular awareness activities. The campaign combines education, reusable products and follow-up so support can create lasting change rather than a one-time distribution.

Sona Tripura's Story

Sona Tripura

From Heladhulapara to public university — and back to serve her community.

Education opened a door. She wants to open more.

Sona Tripura is a young woman from Heladhulapara village in Bhaibonchhara, Khagrachhari. She became the first woman from her village to attend a public university — a milestone that reflects determination, family support and the power of opportunity.

Growing up in a remote community, Sona saw how limited access to menstrual health information and products can affect confidence, comfort, mobility and participation in education and everyday life. She now wants to use her experience and voice to help girls and women in her own community.

This campaign is designed with the community, not only for the community — combining practical menstrual products with awareness, follow-up and measurable learning.
